# Implement the crafting grid for Casualties: Unknown

Build a working in-game mod for the retail version of **Casualties: Unknown**. Opening crafting with the game's normal binding (default **C**) should bring up the grid in the accompanying `crafting-reference.html`. I want to recognize a recipe by its icon and location, point at it to see everything the craft needs, and click **once** to attempt one craft.

**Open the reference and interact with it before implementing.** The grid below its header is the visual and interaction target. The header exists to exercise the sample: omit “Crafting,” the sample INT selector, the craft-count checkbox, and “Reset sample” from the in-game grid. The in-game count preference belongs in mod settings. Use the reference for appearance, placement, and pointer behavior. Use the **installed game** for recipes, assets, player state, crafting rules, and outcomes. Complete the implementation, build it, and exercise the normal game entry point wherever the environment permits.

## Experience to preserve

- **Spatial memory.** I should be able to go straight to a familiar recipe even if I lack an ingredient or my INT has changed. Level is the strongest organizing principle. Category gives a predictable neighborhood within a level. Moderate movement after a content update is fine. A save-specific slot database or a packing optimizer would add complexity I don't need.
- **One glance, one click.** At ordinary desktop resolution and normal UI scale, the base-game catalog should fit at once. The grid replaces the stock recipe list, search, and separate recipe-information pane. Keep the surrounding game UI and menu lifecycle. Hover is inspection, never a prerequisite selection click.
- **A quiet grid.** The art carries most of the information. Color answers a local question when inspecting a recipe. The grid has no persistent category outlines, tier headings, grid lines, legend, text descriptions, instruction strip, or success feed. Avoid adding search, filters, sorting controls, dependent-recipe trees, bulk crafting, or an intermediate confirmation screen.

## Layout and muscle memory

Use square, fixed-size cells with a small consistent gap (reference **2 px**). The low-INT end starts at the left. Lay level bands side by side, each one or more columns wide. A band starts at the **top** of its strip. Inside it, fill each category left-to-right, then downward; start the next category on a fresh row local to that band. The category order in the reference is **Food, Materials, Medicine, Tools, Utilities**. Food therefore starts at the top of every band that contains food. Category boundaries can fall at different heights in adjacent bands. There are no global rectangular category lanes.

When sparse levels share a band, group **by category first**, then by level within that category. Stacking a whole second level partway down recreates the original problem where food appears halfway down the board. Inside equal level/category groups, use deterministic ordering. Match the reference's alphabetical order for the baseline English catalog, with a stable identifier to break ties. Keep ordering independent of inventory, craftability, discovery, hover, or current translation wherever a canonical sort key is available. Reserve positions for normal locked/unknown recipes so they reveal in place, while respecting any native rules for genuinely secret content.

The bundled fixture has **137 recipe entries and 135 distinct outputs**. At desktop width it gives **17 columns × 10 rows**. These are regression targets for that fixture. Detect the runtime catalog size, additional INT levels, and additional categories.

| Reference INT group | Columns |
| --- | ---: |
| 0–3, 4, 5, 6, 7 | 1 per group |
| 8, 9, 10 | 2 per group |
| 11 | 1 |
| 12, 13–15 | 2 per group |
| 16–18 | 1 |

A simple **data-driven layout rule** has been checked against the fixture and gives exactly those bands. For a prospective band and width `w`, its height is the sum over categories of `ceil(number of entries in category / w)`. Walk populated INT levels in ascending order. A level with at least seven recipes gets its own band. Combine consecutive sparser levels while their combined height at two columns is at most ten. Flush a group at a gap or a dense level. For each finished band choose the smallest positive width fitting the ten-row target. If content mods introduce more than ten nonempty categories in a band, raise its height target enough to fit at least one row per category. Seven and ten are **layout tuning values**. The recipe data and level ranges come from the runtime catalog.

Whenever possible, preserve whole bands as the available width changes. The reference uses roughly 37 px as its minimum desktop cell size and wraps whole bands onto another strip on smaller screens. Scale in native canvas units with the user's UI scale. On genuinely oversized modded catalogs, allow overflow before icons become unreadable or unreachable. Avoid repacking on hover. Keep the first stable layout; omit the earlier experimental “lift one row” pass.

## Drawing and inspecting a tile

- **Idle, craftable.** Show the native icon at full color and contrast on the plain crafting surface. This includes attempts the game permits with risk. Use the game’s actual sprite rectangle, preserve its aspect, center it, and constrain it to the cell. The reference sprite occupies about **94% of the square**. Atlases, non-square sprites, and transparent padding must not cause overflow or a microscopic icon.
- **Idle, unavailable.** Use grayscale art at about **18% opacity** for known recipes that cannot currently be attempted. They remain inspectable. **Unknown** recipes show a plain subdued **×** in their reserved square. Hover may identify it generically as unknown. Keep its actual identity, category, INT, output, related recipes, and requirements concealed.
- **Inspecting a known recipe.** Give the inspected tile a soft fill in **its own category color**. Give each relevant known ingredient-producing recipe its own category fill as well. The hovered tile, matching recipe tiles, and temporary ingredient tiles all use the **same tint function**. In particular, avoid a separate gray hover panel. Unrelated tiles keep their normal brightness. Icons stay exactly the same size and position on hover. The reference lifts only the inspected blocked sprite to about 78% opacity, retaining grayscale so the item is recognizable.
- **Within the inspected square**, show `INT 8` or the actual requirement at **top left**. Green means the level is met, yellow means a risky attempt is level-permitted, and red means the level prevents the attempt. Determine this from the native level/risk rules independently of ingredient shortages. At **bottom right**, show output per single craft, e.g. `×5` or `100 mL`. Respect repair/variant semantics and native units. Both labels are inspection-only. Keep them readable without shrinking the icon.
- **Tooltip.** Show localized `Recipe name · Category` immediately on pointer entry or equivalent native focus. It is one short line **always directly above** the tile, including on the top row. Leave a line of clearance there even though the demo header is removed. Center it horizontally and clamp at the left/right edges. Use the game font, a contrasting text shadow, a transparent background, no hover delay or fade, and no pointer interception. The same placement applies to ingredient names. Preserve normal close/back input and appropriate menu focus.
- **Color reference.** Blend the category color at roughly **18%** into the common panel surface. In the dark reference the surface is `#232623`. Food is `#EEC48C`, Materials `#8BC5A2`, Medicine `#E5A0A9`, Tools `#9BBFE0`, and Utilities `#C2A1D1`. Those are pigments before blending. Use native category metadata first. For a raw input with no category, reuse its producing recipe’s category where possible, then infer from unambiguous game quality groups or uses. The reference uses the Materials tint for ambiguous raw supplies. Treat this as a visual fallback only.

## One ingredient group per inspected recipe

Show **all direct requirements together** in vacant cells of the existing grid: crafted inputs, raw resources, liquids, catalysts or retained tools, and items needed for repairs. This is the bill for **one** attempt. Each entry has its own icon, soft category background, and small bottom-right badge. Show only the **required quantity** when it is available. Show **eligible owned/needed in red** when short, including `0/2`. Keep both numbers legible. An ingredient with a recipe gets an entry in this group just like a raw ingredient. Its permanent recipe tile may also receive a background, but **never put the required quantity on that permanent tile**.

Use the game's ingredient matcher and inventory scope. “Owned” means a usable amount for that requirement after respecting other slots in the same craft. Handle stacks, alternatives, specific items competing with wildcard slots, retained versus spent tools, condition thresholds, damaged items for repairs, liquid units, concentrations, and container constraints wherever the native game applies them. For example, if the game requires one qualifying vessel, two unqualified half-full bottles cannot make the tile appear satisfied. A tool counted in one role cannot also be spent in another if the native craft reserves it.

For interchangeable slots, preserve the logical requirement. If the native allocation supplies it, show the actual provider or providers and amount to be used. If it is short, show one generic quality/alternative tile with its eligible total and requirement in comparable units. A list of alternatives must never read like a shopping list requiring one of everything. Known producers of valid alternatives can get a subtle background. Exclude the inspected recipe itself and repair-only routes that cannot produce usable stock for this slot. Unlearned producer recipes stay anonymous. In ingredient tooltips put the name first; include a short qualifier when it matters, e.g. `retained tool`, a condition limit, or a liquid amount. Ingredient tiles are for inspection. Clicking one must not accidentally craft the recipe underneath it.

Use a **single, contiguous group** so my eyes know where to look. The attached `makeIngredientSpace` is an executable reference. Take the occupied recipe positions as fixed, find edge-connected pockets of empty cells, and favor a compact patch far from permanent icons. Choose a consistent starting area for the current grid; smaller bills should reuse its first cells. Look for another existing pocket before growing the grid. Add the minimum rows at the bottom only if no existing pocket can hold the whole group. Added rows belong only to the inspection and never move a permanent recipe. Keep the current group alive as the pointer crosses blank space to reach an ingredient; update it immediately on entering another recipe and clear it on leaving the grid or closing the menu.

## Optional bottom-left craft count

Add one persistent mod preference, e.g. `ShowCraftCounts = false`. **Default off.** The ordinary in-game grid has no count toggle or settings bar. When off, omit the bottom-left badge entirely and skip the repeated-craft calculation. Ingredient quantities, red shortages, the hovered output yield, and craftability still work.

When on, show a plain bottom-left number on every known recipe with at least one currently funded attempt. Zero stays blank. This number means “how many times could I click this particular recipe with what I already have?” Each recipe gets its own independent estimate against the current inventory. It is distinct from output per attempt, items owned, required quantity, and success probability. A risky recipe's number is the number of **attempts**. Do not multiply by yield or assume crafting intermediate recipes. Prefer a native side-effect-free count if it matches that definition; otherwise evaluate repeat allocations on a snapshot, accounting for shared inputs, discrete vessels, and tool wear. Never call the real craft method to estimate a count. Refresh after relevant changes, including when spending an ingredient changes the count of a different recipe.
